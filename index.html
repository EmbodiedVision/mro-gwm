Learning Action-Conditional and Object-Centric Gaussian Splatting World Models for Rigid Objects

Jens U. Kreber, Lukas Mack, Joerg Stueckler
Intelligent Perception in Technical Systems Group
University of Augsburg, Germany
arXiv Paper

Abstract

World models enable intelligent agents to predict the consequences of their actions on the environment. In this paper, we propose Multi Rigid Object Gaussian World Model (MRO-GWM), a novel model that learns action-conditional dynamics of rigid objects in 3D. Using object-centric Gaussians, we can represent arbitrary object shapes and multi-object scenes. We develop a novel temporal-spatial transformer architecture that predicts future rigid body motion from a history of object Gaussians and future actions. Objects are represented by Gaussians in an object-centric frame, which allows to describe object motion as a rigid body transformation. Our model is trained on reconstructions from multiple viewpoints, which requires the model to also handle partial observations of objects due to occlusions. We analyze prediction performance of our approach on synthetic datasets composed of typical household objects with multi-object dynamics and interactions by a robot end effector. We also evaluate our model in model-predictive control for non-prehensile manipulation in simulation and for image-based tracking and prediction of object motion in simulated and real-world scenes.

Method Overview

Gaussian representations are obtained by object-centric Gaussian splatting. The Gaussians are efficiently encoded in anchors. The Gaussian-based shape representations are rigidly transformed by the history of recent object and end-effector poses as input to a transformer that predicts the future object poses. Additionally, we condition the dynamics prediction on the future robot end-effector poses as actions by also inputting their Gaussian-based shape representation into the transformer.


Dynamics prediction transformer. Top left: temporal and temporal-spatial attention relates all spatially corresponding tokens along the temporal dimensions. Bottom left: spatial attention relates tokens at the same time step which are in a local spatial neighborhood. Right: temporal-spatial transformer: Spatial grid pooling and spatial attention blocks are combined with temporal attention and a newly proposed temporal-spatial attention layer. Given a history and future end-effector poses, future object poses are predicted. \(\hat{\tau}^\text{obj}_i\) indicate predicted poses and \(\tau^\text{obj}_\emptyset\) a fixed dummy pose.

Real-World Tracking

We capture multi-view RGB-D images and obtain poses with ORB-SLAM3 and object masks with SAM3. We track each object with its Gaussian representation using FoundationPose (right) and compare to the ground truth video (left).

Object poses are accurately tracked with minor noisy perturbations.

Real-World Prediction

We use our real-world tracking pipeline to obtain a history of object poses and directly apply a model trained in simulation to predict future poses over horizon 2.4s. We show predictions on the right and ground-truth video on the left.

Initially, plausible interactions between objects and the end-effector are predicted. At later steps in the second scene, the model misses the interaction between box and end effector, presumably as prediction gets out of distribution due to compounding prediction errors or insufficient training data.
